Two ways to sell the kit

Start with the easy upgrade: retailer-specific digital coupons. Then add the larger S&H-style model: your own neighborhood stamp token funded by retailers and redeemed through your catalog.

Track A — Retailer Digital Coupon Tokens

Replace a retailer’s paper coupon with a branded KRC-20 coupon token. The retailer defines the discount rules, and the customer redeems the token through that retailer.

  • One retailer or one campaign per token
  • Coupon Broadcaster for weekly distribution
  • Burn-on-redeem proof
  • Best first pilot for existing coupon clients

Track B — Neighborhood Stamp Network

Your agency operates a local rewards token. Retailers buy the tokens from you, distribute them to shoppers, and customers redeem them in your online prize catalog.

  • Retailer-funded reward inventory
  • Catalog redemption center
  • Reserve and float model
  • Highest upside for recurring revenue

The neighborhood stamp flow

This is the digital version of the classic trading-stamp model: retailers fund the rewards, customers accumulate tokens over time, and your business operates the redemption catalog.

1. Retailer buys

Retailers buy your branded stamp tokens as a loyalty and advertising expense.

2. Retailer distributes

Coupon Broadcaster sends token rewards to customer wallet lists based on weekly spend.

3. Customer saves

Customers see their reward balance grow in a KRC-20 wallet instead of a paper book.

4. Catalog redeems

Your prize catalog accepts only your stamp token for selected rewards and merchandise.

5. Token burns

Redeemed tokens are burned or retired so the same rewards cannot be used twice.

Why retailers take the meeting

Retailers do not need to understand the chain first. They need a stronger loyalty offer, better repeat-visit incentive, and a reason for customers to keep paying attention after the weekly coupon expires.

More than a coupon

Customers can accumulate value across visits, which gives retailers a reason to run recurring campaigns instead of one-off discounts.

Funded like advertising

Easy first pilot

Start with a small subscriber list, one retailer, one reward token, one campaign rule, and a simple redemption offer.

Rewards-first guardrails

The strongest starting model is rewards and catalog redemption, not cash-out or investment language. The kit is designed for coupons, stamps, loyalty, and prize catalogs. Legal and tax review should be completed before live rollout.

Recommended

  • Redeem for catalog goods, services, discounts, or merchant-defined rewards.
  • Burn or retire tokens when redeemed.
  • Maintain a redemption reserve for outstanding rewards.
  • Publish clear campaign terms and reward availability.

Avoid at launch

  • Cash redemption or guaranteed cash value.
  • Promises that tokens will rise in value.
  • Operating the catalog like a crypto exchange.
  • Unlimited prize liability without reserve accounting.

Suggested 30-day pilot

The first goal is not to rebuild every coupon program. The first goal is to prove that retailers will buy a digital reward campaign and customers can receive, save, and redeem tokens without confusion.

3 retailers

Pick friendly merchants that already run coupons or customer specials.

1 campaign

Use one token drop rule, such as rewards based on weekly spend.

50–250 users

Start with an opt-in list before expanding to a broader local audience.

1 redemption

Offer one simple reward so the campaign is easy to explain and measure.
